How Much Does a 10Gbps Dedicated Server Cost? Hardware, Bandwidth, and Extra Fees

Likhon Ahmed Verified Author

Founder & CEO, A1 Technovation • Infrastructure & Technical SEO Architect

Enterprise rack-mount servers configured with 10Gbps SFP+ connectivity, high-density memory, and NVMe flash storage.

A 10Gbps dedicated server can cost a few hundred dollars per month, but the price depends on the hardware and network service included. Published unmetered examples reviewed for this guide include packages at $499, $596, and $690 per month. These are individual offers, not a market-wide price range.

Atal Networks, for example, lists its ATAL10G package at $690 per month, with 64GB RAM, 1TB SSD storage, five IPv4 addresses, and 10Gbps unmetered bandwidth. A traffic-capped server may cost less upfront, while management, licenses, additional IPs, and setup charges can increase the final bill.

The useful question is the complete cost of a server that meets your requirements. We can calculate that by separating the base package, network charges, and optional services.

Research Metadata: Pricing research checked November 4, 2026. Examples use USD and draw mainly from US-market offers. Prices depend on the selected deployment and configuration; they do not apply to every region. This guide uses published provider information without independent performance testing.

Starting Prices and Add-Ons Need Separate Treatment

Some advertised prices describe a category of servers rather than a complete selected configuration. Others cover only the bandwidth option.

For example:

  • Sharktech advertises configurations from $219 per month with 10Gbps connectivity, 300TB of monthly traffic, and 2TB NVMe starting storage. The selected CPU and RAM still need checking. (See Sharktech USA profile)
  • SERVER.NET lists a $33 monthly traffic option added to the server price, with 100TB of transfer, burst capacity up to 10Gbps, and $2 per additional TB.

The $33 option is not a complete server rental. The $219 starting offer is not an unmetered package. Keeping these distinctions visible prevents a misleading price comparison.

Separate the Server Price From the Network Price

A provider may bundle everything into one price or charge for hardware and networking separately. We should identify what each line includes before adding up the quote.

Hardware and Hosting

The base rental commonly covers the physical processor, memory, storage, and supporting hosting services. It may also include remote access, IP addresses, and a default network package.

Hardware choices affect the price:

  • Processor model, generation, and socket count: High single-core performance vs high multi-thread density.
  • Memory capacity and type: DDR4 ECC vs DDR5 ECC memory channels.
  • Drive type, number, and capacity: SATA HDD vs Enterprise SATA SSD vs PCIe Gen4/Gen5 NVMe flash.
  • RAID configuration and usable storage: Hardware RAID controllers vs software mirrors.
  • Specialized components: Hardware accelerators such as enterprise GPUs.

Compare the workload requirements before comparing core counts. A newer processor with fewer cores may suit an application better than an older dual-socket server.

Storage also needs careful treatment. Two 1TB drives do not necessarily provide 2TB of usable space. A mirrored configuration uses one drive’s capacity to maintain a second copy.

Public Port and Bandwidth Commitment

A 10Gbps port describes the connection’s nominal capacity. The purchased bandwidth commitment describes the service the provider agrees to supply.

A plan can include a 10Gbps port while guaranteeing a lower rate. It may permit bursts above that commitment under defined conditions.

Request these details separately:

Network detail Information needed
Public port Connection speed available for internet traffic
Guaranteed rate Bandwidth covered by the service agreement
Burst capacity Permitted rate above the commitment
Connection terms Dedicated or shared capacity conditions
Private network Separate capacity for internal traffic

A fast private connection or regional exchange connection does not establish the same capacity for general internet traffic.

Monthly Transfer

Transfer describes the amount of data moved during the billing period, usually measured in GB or TB.

Confirm whether the provider counts:

  • Outbound traffic only.
  • Inbound and outbound traffic together.
  • The greater of the two directions.
  • Usage across a shared allowance.

Also check whether backup, private-network, or other service traffic uses the same allowance.

Compare the Three Main Bandwidth Billing Models

1 Metered Transfer

A metered plan includes a defined allowance and may charge for additional data.

For example, a package might include 100TB per month and charge $2 for each extra TB. At 150TB of billable usage, the excess charge would be:

Metered Overage Calculation 50TB × $2 = $100

The actual result depends on the provider’s accounting, rounding, and billing rules. Some services reduce the connection rate or require an upgrade instead of charging automatic overage.

Metered plans can suit workloads with short bursts and moderate total transfer.

2 Unmetered Bandwidth

An unmetered package does not bill against a fixed monthly TB allowance under its stated terms. The connection rate, purchased commitment, and usage policy still apply.

This model can make spending easier to plan for sustained file delivery, streaming, or other transfer-heavy services.

It does not promise infinite speed or line-rate application performance. Storage, processor load, routing, and the receiving system can still limit throughput.

3 95th-Percentile Billing

A 95th-percentile plan calculates a billable bandwidth value from samples collected during the billing period. Under a common method, the provider removes the highest 5% of samples and uses the next highest value.

The contract determines the sampling interval, traffic direction, minimum commitment, and excess rate.

Monthly transfer alone cannot predict this bill. Two services can move the same number of terabytes but produce different sampled rates because their traffic arrives at different times.

Billing model Main cost driver Best comparison input
Metered Billable transfer above an allowance Monthly GB or TB
Unmetered Purchased package and service terms Sustained and peak requirements
95th percentile Sampled bandwidth under the contract A representative traffic time series

Account for Licenses, IPs, and Management

A low base price may exclude services required to operate the application.

IP Addresses

Check the number of usable IPv4 addresses included. A subnet’s total address count does not always equal the number available for applications.

Ask about additional-address fees, allocation conditions, and any routing charges. Confirm IPv6 availability and the included allocation separately.

Do not assume that a provider’s standalone IP-leasing price applies to addresses attached to a rented server.

Operating Systems and Control Panels

A commercial operating system or control panel may add recurring charges. The cost can depend on the edition, licensing basis, account tier, or server type.

Request the exact license needed for the deployment. A license intended for a virtual server may differ from one intended for physical hardware.

Even when the operating system has no license fee, paid support or management may remain separate.

Server Management

24×7 support does not automatically include full server management.

Hardware support, operating-system updates, firewall changes, application troubleshooting, and backup administration are different tasks.

An itemized management quote should define:

  • Tasks the provider performs.
  • Monitoring and response coverage.
  • Supported operating systems and applications.
  • Included administration time.
  • Charges for work outside the service scope.

Include internal staff time in the operating budget if your team will manage the server.

Check the Remaining Fees

Cost item Detail to confirm
Setup Standard installation, custom-build, and provisioning charges
Backups Storage, retention, restores, and transfer charges
DDoS protection Included coverage, optional services, and traffic treatment
Storage upgrades Drive price, RAID options, and replacement coverage
Migration Labor, data transfer, and overlapping server rentals
Renewal Price after an introductory period

A missing charge on a product page is not proof that the service is free. Mark it as unconfirmed until the quote resolves it.

Calculate the Full Monthly Cost

Use this formula:

Complete Monthly Cost Formula Monthly cost = base package + separate network fees + transfer overage + IPs + licenses + management + backups

Count each service once. If the base package already includes the network service or IP allocation, do not add it again.

For annual planning:

First-Year Total Cost of Ownership First-year cost = one-time fees + recurring charges for months 1–12 + usage charges + applicable taxes

A promotional first month should not become the assumed rate for the whole year. Apply the renewal price from the month it takes effect.

For hourly bare metal, multiply the hourly rate by the planned billable hours. Then check minimum commitments, monthly caps, and charges that continue while the system is stopped. Powering off a server may not end the rental.

That is why the excess-transfer rate belongs in the first comparison, not at the end of the purchasing process.

Budget for Busy Months

A costly month does not automatically make unmetered hosting the cheaper annual option.

Using the same hypothetical plans, assume:

  • Ten months at 80TB.
  • Two months at 500TB.
  • One $50 setup charge.

Metered First-Year Cost

10 normal months + 2 peak spike months

(10 × $250) + (2 × $1,050) + $50 = $4,650

Total: $4,650

Unmetered First-Year Cost

12 flat rate unmetered months

12 × $650 + $50 = $7,850

Total: $7,850

The metered example costs $3,200 less over the year, even though its two busy months cost more.

Unmetered service may still offer budget certainty or other suitable terms. The decision should account for expected annual usage, the risk of unexpected traffic, and equivalent network commitments.

Use a normal month, a busy month, and a plausible high-usage month in the budget.

Estimate Transfer From Bandwidth Use

Port speed and monthly transfer use different units.

  • Gbps measures data rate in gigabits per second.
  • GB and TB measure data amounts.
  • Eight bits equal one byte.

At the theoretical rate:

Theoretical Line-Rate Conversion 10Gbps ÷ 8 = 1.25GB per second
Usage pattern Theoretical transfer
10Gbps for one hour 4.5TB
10Gbps for one hour daily over 30 days 135TB
10Gbps continuously for 30 days 3.24PB
100TB spread across 30 days About 309Mbps average

These figures use decimal units, cover one direction, and exclude protocol overhead.

A workload can average about 309Mbps across a month yet need a much faster port during short transfer windows. The average helps estimate usage; the peak helps size the connection.

Full duplex also does not mean a single download runs at 20Gbps. A 10Gbps full-duplex connection supports sending and receiving at the link rate in each direction, subject to the service and path.

Spend on the Resource That Limits the Workload

A larger network package helps only when the application can use it.

  • File hosting and downloads: check sustained storage reads, cache behavior, and concurrent requests. Include enough usable capacity for the content library.
  • Backups: compare the source read rate, destination write rate, compression load, and permitted transfer window.
  • Streaming: separate delivery from encoding. Serving stored video and converting live video can require very different processors.
  • Virtualization: account for combined guest traffic, memory demand, and storage I/O.
  • SaaS and APIs: check database response time and CPU use before attributing slow requests to bandwidth.

A suitable processor, enough RAM, and the right storage layout can matter more than a larger advertised port.

Account for Deployment Region

This pricing method applies globally, but a provider may quote different prices for different deployments.

Hardware availability, network service, delivery terms, billing currency, and applicable taxes can change the total. The cheapest deployment also may not meet the application’s latency or operating requirements.

Request the same configuration in each suitable region. Keep the original currency visible, or state the date and rate used for any conversion.

Do not assume that one published price applies to all locations listed on a provider’s website.

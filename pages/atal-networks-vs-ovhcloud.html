Atal Networks vs OVHcloud: Network, Pricing and Support

A dedicated server’s monthly price tells only part of the story. Two offers can include similar memory and storage while providing different bandwidth commitments, support access, and recovery options.

Likhon Ahmed
Published October 27, 2026 • Infrastructure Systems Review
Bare metal architecture, public unmetered throughput, and support comparison Verified Specifications: Oct 27, 2026

A dedicated server’s monthly price tells only part of the story. Two offers can include similar memory and storage while providing different bandwidth commitments, support access, and recovery options. Those differences affect both operating costs and the work required to keep applications available.

Atal Networks and OVHcloud should be compared at the configuration and location level. Atal Networks publishes dedicated-server packages with defined transfer allowances, unmetered options, and bundled IPv4 addresses. OVHcloud offers several hardware ranges with different networking features and support tiers. Neither provider is the better choice for every workload.

This comparison focuses on dedicated servers. It examines published specifications and service terms checked on October 27, 2026. It does not present hands-on benchmarks or measured support performance.

Atal Networks vs OVHcloud at a Glance

The most useful starting point is the complete service package: hardware, network capacity, traffic conditions, support, and recurring charges.

Factor Atal Networks OVHcloud
Dedicated-server selection Published Xeon-based packages alongside custom configuration options Multiple ranges, including ECO, Advance, Scale, and High Grade
Traffic options Fixed transfer allowances and advertised unmetered packages Unmetered offers with product-specific and regional exceptions
Network comparison Confirm the included public rate and port configuration Separate public bandwidth from private vRack capacity
Support Published SLA describes 24/7 access and initial-response terms Included Standard support and paid tiers; arrangements differ by market
Availability terms Separate published network and power commitments Commitments vary by server range and contracting region
Main buying task Confirm the exact hardware, location, and included service scope Select the appropriate range, region, bandwidth, and support tier

These are provider-level distinctions. The final comparison needs an exact server offer from each company.

Compare Equivalent Servers Before Comparing Providers

A meaningful comparison starts with a workload requirement.

For example, a business might need 64GB of RAM, mirrored SSD storage, five IPv4 addresses, and enough public bandwidth to distribute large files. Another might need strong single-core performance and low latency for a game server. The cheapest suitable configuration could differ between those workloads.

Before comparing prices, record:

  • Exact CPU model and generation.
  • RAM capacity.
  • Drive type, quantity, and usable capacity.
  • Server location.
  • Included public bandwidth and traffic allowance.
  • Required IP addresses.
  • Support and management scope.
  • Billing period and setup fees.

Keep the customer storefront separate from the server location. A server’s physical location does not, by itself, identify the support contract or commercial terms that apply.

Also distinguish advertised coverage from available inventory. A location page does not prove that the required CPU, storage layout, and bandwidth package are ready to order there.

Network: Port Speed, Traffic and Routing

Network terms can change the value of a dedicated server more than a small difference in its base price.

Port speed is not the same as guaranteed throughput

A physical network port describes the capacity of an interface. It does not automatically establish how much internet bandwidth the provider commits to delivering.

Buyers should distinguish five things:

Term What it describes
Port speed The interface’s maximum link rate
Included public bandwidth The external network rate supplied with the service
Guaranteed bandwidth The rate covered by the provider’s specific commitment
Burst capacity Additional capacity available temporarily under stated conditions
Actual throughput The transfer rate achieved between particular endpoints

A 10Gbps interface can operate with a lower public bandwidth allocation. Even where a provider commits to a public rate, transfers can encounter limits elsewhere, including the receiving network, storage system, application, or route.

Atal’s published ATAL10G details require confirmation before purchase: its dedicated-server page contains a 10Gbps unmetered description alongside a 1Gbps port field, while its homepage lists a 10Gbps port. Buyers should obtain the ordered specification in writing.

OVHcloud separately publishes public and private bandwidth. Its private vRack capacity supports communication between connected services; it should not be treated as equivalent internet bandwidth when comparing offers.

Unmetered traffic still needs a defined rate

Unmetered bandwidth generally means that data transfer is not billed against a fixed monthly volume under the selected offer. It does not mean unlimited transmission speed.

For either provider, establish:

  • Whether transfer accounting includes incoming traffic, outgoing traffic, or both.
  • Whether a monthly allowance applies.
  • What happens after an allowance is used.
  • Whether fair-use restrictions affect sustained traffic.
  • Whether guaranteed bandwidth costs extra.

OVHcloud’s documentation identifies Asia-Pacific exceptions to its general unmetered offering. Its Scale documentation, for example, describes a default 1Gbps connection with a 25TB monthly allowance in APAC. Buyers must check the exact range and region rather than apply that example to every OVHcloud server.

Routing matters as much as the location label

A server in the nearest city does not always provide the best connection to users. Internet routing, peering, congestion, and destination networks affect the result.

Before placing a latency-sensitive workload, request a test endpoint in the proposed location. Measure from the networks that customers actually use, ideally at several times of day.

Useful measurements include round-trip latency, packet loss, jitter, and sustained transfer performance. A single speed test cannot establish long-term network quality.

For file delivery, sustained throughput may dominate the decision. For gaming or interactive applications, latency consistency can matter more than a larger advertised port.

Hardware and Performance: Look Beyond the Processor Brand

“Intel Xeon” and “AMD EPYC” describe broad processor families. They do not identify the performance of a specific configuration.

Compare exact processor models, generations, core counts, and memory arrangements. An older dual-socket server may provide many threads but still be less suitable for a workload that depends heavily on individual-core performance.

OVHcloud’s ECO range gives buyers alternatives to its newer server ranges. That matters when comparing an older Atal configuration: selecting only a newer, higher-priced OVHcloud offer could produce a misleading value comparison.

Storage deserves the same attention. A “1TB disk” description does not establish that the drive is NVMe, that storage is mirrored, or that backups are included.

Record the drive type, number of drives, RAID arrangement, and usable capacity. Two 1TB drives in RAID 1 provide approximately one drive’s capacity before filesystem overhead, rather than 2TB of usable mirrored storage.

RAID also does not replace a backup. It may help maintain service through a drive failure, but it does not protect against every deletion, compromise, or application error.

Without comparable workload testing, specifications support a suitability assessment, not a claim that one provider is universally faster.

Pricing: Compare the Complete Cost

The relevant price is the cost of meeting the workload requirement.

Atal’s published offers include a $99 monthly server with 50TB of transfer, a $150 offer with 100TB, and a $168 advertised 1Gbps unmetered package. These are listed package prices, not confirmed quotes for every location or custom configuration.

OVHcloud’s catalog spans different hardware generations and server ranges. Its published price listings also show setup fees on some configurations. Buyers should therefore compare the selected order, including its commitment and options, rather than a provider-wide “starting from” price.

Include setup fees and required extras

Build the comparison using the same currency, tax basis, location requirements, and billing period.

Cost component What to verify
Base server Exact hardware and monthly charge
Setup One-time fee and any commitment needed to waive it
Bandwidth Included rate and required upgrades
Transfer Allowance, overage charges, or applicable restrictions
IP addresses Included allocation and recurring additions
Backup Storage, software, retention, and restoration costs
Licensing Operating system and control-panel charges
Support Included tier and optional upgrade
Management OS and application work covered by the contract

Do not assume that an unlisted charge is zero. Record it as unconfirmed until the quote or applicable terms resolve it.

Calculate first-month and annual costs separately

Use two simple calculations:

  • First-month cost = setup fees + monthly server fee + required recurring extras.
  • Twelve-month cost = one-time charges + all recurring charges across the twelve-month period.

A hypothetical $150 monthly server with a $150 setup fee costs $1,950 over twelve months before extras. A hypothetical $165 monthly server without setup costs $1,980. The apparent monthly saving becomes only $30 over the year.

Those figures illustrate the calculation; they are not matched quotes from these providers.

Account-level support changes the calculation

OVHcloud US Business support is priced at 10% of the monthly infrastructure bill, with a $250 monthly minimum before taxes. Its support documentation states that support attaches to the account and applies to the services within it.

That structure matters. A support plan can represent a substantial addition for one server, while a larger deployment spreads the account-level charge across multiple services. Do not add the full account charge independently to every server.

For a broader operating-cost assessment, include administration and migration effort. Keep estimates separate from confirmed provider fees.

Support: Availability, Response and Responsibility

Support comparisons become misleading when “24/7” is treated as a complete service description.

Buyers need to know which channels are available, what incidents qualify for priority handling, and what the provider will actually fix.

What the published terms say

Atal’s SLA describes 24/7 availability through email, phone, and chat. It states an initial-response aim of 15–30 minutes and a maximum of 90 minutes. These are published service terms, not independently measured results.

OVHcloud’s US Standard page describes 24/7 support with a first-response objective of three hours for critical P1 incidents. It lists live chat during US business hours. US Business support adds a dedicated 24/7 telephone channel and a 30-minute P1 response objective.

OVHcloud’s worldwide support pages describe different arrangements. Buyers should use documentation applicable to their account and contract rather than combine terms from different markets.

A response is not a resolution

An initial reply might acknowledge an incident, request diagnostics, or begin investigation. It does not establish when a failed component will be replaced or an application restored.

A useful support comparison separates:

  • Time to first response.
  • Time to technical intervention.
  • Hardware replacement or infrastructure recovery.
  • Restoration of the operating system and application.

Published response objectives should not be presented as observed averages. OVHcloud’s US Business page also explicitly states that its listed objectives are not guaranteed timelines.

Infrastructure support is not full server management

A provider can support hardware and network connectivity without managing databases, security updates, or application errors.

Before ordering, establish responsibility for each task:

Task Question to resolve
Physical hardware failure Who diagnoses and replaces the component?
Connectivity failure Which network faults fall within provider support?
OS updates Are patching and reboot coordination included?
Application failures Does support cover the installed software?
Backups Who configures, monitors, and tests them?
Data restoration Who performs recovery and validates the result?

Neither a root-access feature nor a 24/7 support statement answers these management questions.

Reliability: Read the SLA Beyond the Percentage

An SLA defines commitments and remedies. It is not a record of achieved uptime.

Atal publishes 99.99% network and power commitments. OVHcloud’s US dedicated-server SLA sets different commitments by range, including 99.90% for specified ECO and Game services, 99.95% for Advance and certain other ranges, and 99.99% for Scale and High Grade.

The percentages alone do not establish which service is more reliable. The covered components, outage definitions, exclusions, and claim requirements also matter.

Compare:

  • What qualifies as downtime.
  • When the downtime clock starts.
  • Which maintenance or external events are excluded.
  • How credits are calculated.
  • When a claim must be submitted.

For example, Atal’s published claim process specifies a 30-day reporting period. OVHcloud’s US dedicated-server SLA specifies 60 days and defines unavailability around external connectivity, with timing linked to creation of a support case.

DDoS protection needs workload-specific evaluation

An included DDoS feature does not, by itself, establish equivalent protection.

Ask about the relevant protocols, filtering controls, attack-handling policies, and any conditions under which traffic may be restricted. A game server, public website, and VPN endpoint may need different protection characteristics.

Similarly, hardware replacement is only one stage of recovery. Replacing a failed disk does not automatically restore its data.

Business-critical applications should have an explicit recovery plan, tested backups, and suitable redundancy. A single dedicated server remains a single failure point regardless of the provider’s headline SLA.

Deployment, Management and Expansion

Day-to-day management features can reduce recovery time and administrative effort.

Check the selected service for:

  • Remote console access.
  • Rescue environments.
  • OS installation and reinstallation.
  • Custom installation-media support.
  • API operations.
  • Monitoring and status information.
  • Private networking.

Missing public documentation should be treated as a question to resolve, not proof that a feature is unavailable.

Consider upgrades before the server reaches capacity. More RAM may require a maintenance window. A different CPU or storage layout may require moving to another machine. A bandwidth upgrade may depend on the location and product.

For multi-server applications, evaluate how private networking connects application servers, databases, and backup systems. Private capacity should be assessed against those internal workloads rather than added to the public bandwidth figure.

Which Provider Fits Your Workload?

The practical choice depends on the configuration that meets the requirement, not a universal provider ranking.

Workload Most important checks
File delivery and streaming Sustained public bandwidth, transfer terms, destination routing, storage throughput
SaaS and business applications CPU suitability, storage latency, recovery design, support scope
Gaming Per-core performance, player latency, jitter, relevant DDoS filtering
Virtualization and hosting resale RAM capacity, storage layout, IP allocation, private networking
VPN and proxy infrastructure Permitted use, address requirements, routing, encryption performance, traffic terms
Small technical teams Management coverage, escalation access, backups, restoration responsibility

When Atal Networks belongs on the shortlist

Atal Networks is worth evaluating when its published bandwidth packages and bundled resources align with the workload. Buyers can use the Atal Networks dedicated-server listings to identify a starting configuration, then request confirmation of the exact hardware, location, public bandwidth, and complete price.

Its published support commitments also warrant consideration. Their practical value depends on the required scope of assistance and the applicable service agreement, rather than response-time wording alone.

When OVHcloud belongs on the shortlist

OVHcloud is worth evaluating when the buyer wants to compare several server generations and ranges or needs documented private-networking options. Its ECO and newer ranges should be considered according to hardware requirements and budget, rather than treating the company as one uniform server package.

Its paid support tiers may also suit teams that need defined escalation channels. The account-level cost belongs in the comparison.

When neither selected package is sufficient

A basic dedicated-server package may not meet requirements for fully managed applications, automated failover, or recovery across separate locations.

In those cases, compare an architecture and management arrangement. The provider decision comes after identifying the necessary servers, backups, redundancy, and operational responsibilities.

Questions to Ask Before Ordering

Send both providers the same requirements and request written answers:

  • Which exact CPU, drives, and RAM will be delivered?
  • Is that configuration available in the required location?
  • What public bandwidth is included and guaranteed?
  • What transfer allowances, restrictions, or overage charges apply?
  • Which IP addresses and licenses are included?
  • What support and management tasks are covered?
  • What are the first-month and twelve-month costs?
  • What happens during hardware failure, cancellation, and migration?

Comparable answers are more useful than comparing two differently configured “starting from” offers.

Recommended Next Steps

Choose the service whose confirmed configuration and terms fit the workload. A lower monthly price, larger port number, or higher SLA percentage is useful only when the rest of the package supports the application.
